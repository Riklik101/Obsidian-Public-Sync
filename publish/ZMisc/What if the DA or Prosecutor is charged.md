if the DA or prosecutor is charged, another one will likely be appointed. This rarely happens and thereby is very rarely documented, but a few things are likely to occur. 
* Criminal charges
* Sanctions - disbarment and removal from office
* Review of cases the prosecutor was involved with, and convictions may be overturned if misconduct is found
* Civil lawsuits - misconduct victims could pursue civil lawsuits against the prosecutor
* Contempt - the judge would hold the prosecutor for contempt if the act was done within the courtroom

There are a few things though, like 
* Many prosecutors have absolute immunity from federal civil rights lawsuits
* It does have limits, like failure to disclose exculpatory evidence, although the office cannot be sued for failing to train prosecutors on this duty
* Some states including Texas allow laws to rein in discretion, allowing for removal of attorneys for not prosecuting certain crimes without sufficient justification.
