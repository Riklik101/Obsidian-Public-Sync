A wastewater treatment plant is water that has been changed to being used and contaminated, typically with sewage or industrial waste.

A number of controlled steps are required to treat this water before it can be either released or cleaned further for potable water. 
Importantly, a wastewater treatment plant differents from a water treatment plant; in that a WW treatment plant primarily is situated near water sources or rivers that they can release the treated water into. 

Otherwise, a treatment plant's water can be used for non-potable water usage, such as irrigation, industrial use, etc. 

## Process
The process of treating wastewater is complex, however it can be simplified into some basic steps.

### Pre-Treatment
This treatment involves [[Pump Station]]s, screening, the [[Grease Trap]], and grit chambers.

### Primary Treatment
Primary involves the [[Primary Clarifier]], aereation, [[Secondary Clarifier]], gravity thickeners for the sludge, and other miscellaneous steps. 
For brevity, secondary treatment is also included in this step.

### Final Treatment
##### Water
The water is finally treated through filtration, then through a variety of steps; clorination, reverse-osmosis, ozonation, and a combination of these are all popular steps. Lastly is the influent flame and the discharge, although an important step is the monitoring plant which is necessary so that everythign leaving the plant is monitored for quality.

##### Sludge
The sludge is digested through anaerobic digesters that create biogas, typically used to heat the plant itself. Sludge is then dewatered through a press, though some use high temperature screw lifts and other methods, then it is lifted to trucks to be disposed of; some plants are working to use it to extract elements, and this sludge can be used as fertilizer or for other methods as it is santized through the dewatering process generally.
